# Deep Research Brief 2: Competitor Gap Pass (Free, Club-Native & Emerging Tools)

## Why this brief exists

A first competitor study covered the traditional **Association Management Software (AMS)** incumbents well (Wild Apricot, ClubExpress, MemberPlanet, Join It, Memberful, Raklet, Mighty Networks) but **completely missed several categories** that threaten our strategy:

1. **Free / donation-tip-funded platforms** — most importantly **Zeffy** (100% free, no platform/transaction/processing fees, US + Canada, 100k+ nonprofits). This category undercuts our planned "fair pricing" differentiation to literally $0.
2. **Volunteer-org-native tools outside the US** — e.g. **TidyHQ, membermojo, Clubforce, SportLoMo, Amilia** — purpose-built for exactly our buyer.
3. **Young / emerging entrants** — recent startups, indie/bootstrapped tools, and newly-funded players the first pass didn't catch. We need to know who is building toward this space *now*, not just the established names.

Because our pilot club (Kootenay Mountaineering Club) is **Canadian**, US-centric research is not enough. We need Canadian and Commonwealth (UK / Ireland / Australia / New Zealand) coverage.

## Product context (who we are)

**Memba** is a planned SaaS membership platform for **volunteer-run membership organizations**. Our long-term market is broad: any organization run by rotating volunteers that has members, dues/renewals, communications, and private content — sports and recreation clubs, hobbyist and special-interest groups, community associations, professional and alumni groups, faith and cultural groups, and similar.

**Our beachhead — where we are deliberately starting — is volunteer-run outdoor/activity clubs** (mountaineering, hiking, skiing, cycling, paddling, climbing, trail-running), roughly 30–300 members, with Kootenay Mountaineering Club as the pilot/design partner. We are starting there because the workflows are concentrated and underserved, not because the product is limited to that niche. **Do not treat outdoor clubs as the ceiling of the market — treat them as the entry point.**

Differentiation hypotheses we are trying to validate or kill:

- First-class **household / family memberships** (one payer, multiple individuals, shared address, individual logins/waivers/profiles, children aging up). Relevant far beyond outdoor clubs.
- **Renewal + payment automation** (calendar-fixed and rolling anniversary; reminders; offline payments; admin corrections).
- **Volunteer-admin simplicity** and easy **handoff** to the next rotating volunteer.
- **Fair, predictable pricing** by *active members* (not total contacts), with **no Memba surcharge** on payments and easy data export ("leave anytime").
- A path toward **lightweight activity/event management** (for clubs that run trips: leader roles, capacity, waitlists, expressions of interest, waivers, emergency contacts, member-only visibility) — one of several vertical depth options, not the whole product.

## Primary research question

For each competitor below, determine: **Could this product credibly replace Memba for a volunteer-run membership organization — starting with a 30–300 member outdoor club in Canada (or the UK/AU/NZ), but also considering broader volunteer orgs? Where does it win, and where does it fail our buyer?** And critically: **does its existence weaken Memba's pricing or domain-model differentiation?**

## Workstream: young & emerging entrants (REQUIRED — this is a priority)

The first research pass was biased toward established players. Explicitly hunt for **new and rising competitors** — the people building in this space right now. Look for:

- **Recent startups** (founded in roughly the last 1–5 years) building membership, club, or association software.
- **Indie / bootstrapped / solo-founder tools** (the same kind of product Memba is) — search Indie Hackers, Product Hunt, Hacker News "Show HN", BetaList, microconf/SaaS communities.
- **Recently funded companies** — search Crunchbase, recent seed/pre-seed/Series A announcements, accelerator batches (Y Combinator, Techstars, Antler, etc.) tagged membership / community / club / nonprofit / SaaS.
- **AI-native newcomers** pitching "AI for nonprofits / community management."
- **Vertical newcomers** aimed at a specific club type (climbing gyms, cycling clubs, paddling, ski touring) that could expand horizontally.
- **Regional newcomers** in Canada, UK, Ireland, AU, NZ.

For each emerging entrant found, capture: who they are, founding year, funding/backing, target customer, traction signals (users, reviews, press), what's differentiated, and whether they are converging on Memba's space. **Flag any that look like a direct future competitor even if they are early/small.**

Also: **actively look for competitors NOT on any list above.** The previous research's blind spots were free tools, non-US tools, and new entrants — hunt all three. Name anything you find.

## For each competitor, capture

- **Headquarters / primary geography** and which countries it actually serves (does it work in Canada? UK? AU/NZ?).
- **Target customer** — what kinds of organizations (sports clubs only? any nonprofit? professional associations?). Note breadth, since our long-term market is broad.
- **Maturity & momentum** — founding year, funding/ownership (bootstrapped, VC, PE, acquired), and trajectory (growing, stagnant, recently pivoted?). We care about both price-hike/stagnation risk in incumbents and disruption risk from newcomers.
- **Pricing model** — exact numbers where possible. Platform fees, transaction fees, payment-processing fees, setup fees, contact/member-count tiers or cliffs, free-tier limits, nonprofit discounts. **For free platforms, explain precisely how they make money** (e.g. optional donor tips) and what the catch is.
- **Core features** vs Memba's scope:
  - Member database & statuses (active/lapsed/prospective)
  - **Family / household memberships** — how well modeled? One bill, multiple logins, shared address, dependents, children aging up? (This is our wedge — be specific and skeptical.)
  - Renewals & recurring payments; reminders; offline payment recording; admin corrections
  - Member directory + per-field privacy controls
  - Member-only / private content
  - Email announcements & deliverability; member-to-member communication
  - **Event / activity / trip management** — leader roles, capacity, waitlists, expressions of interest, approvals, member-only visibility, **waivers**, **emergency contacts**
  - Roles / permissions granularity
  - Data import/export; **Google Sheets** integration; lock-in vs portability
  - Volunteer-admin usability & handoff
  - Mobile (app or responsive)
- **Suitability for volunteer-run organizations** generally, AND specifically for **outdoor clubs** (trips, waivers, households), since that is our beachhead.
- **Known complaints & praise** — pull real quotes with source links from G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Reddit, club/nonprofit forums, app stores.
- **Why an org would choose it; why an org would reject or churn from it.**

## Required deliverables

1. **Gap-competitor matrix** — all competitors above (established + emerging) as rows, the feature/pricing/geography/target-customer/maturity dimensions as columns, with a clear cell for **family/household handling** and **activity/waiver handling**.

2. **Zeffy deep-dive** — a standalone section. Is "free forever" real and sustainable? What do nonprofits actually pay in practice (tip take-rate, conversion)? What does it NOT do that a volunteer org / outdoor club needs (households? trips? waivers? directory privacy? member logins?)? **Assess directly: how much does Zeffy erode Memba's "fair pricing" advantage, and what differentiation survives contact with a free competitor?**

3. **Emerging-entrants briefing** — a standalone section listing the new/young/funded players found, with the traction and convergence assessment above. Call out the 2–3 newcomers most likely to become real threats and why.

4. **Canadian & Commonwealth market note** — which of these tools are genuinely used by volunteer orgs and outdoor/activity clubs in Canada, UK, Ireland, AU, NZ? Any region-specific leaders we should worry about? Payment-surcharge sensitivity by country.

5. **Revised threat ranking** — rank ALL competitors (including the original AMS set: Wild Apricot, ClubExpress, etc.) by threat level to Memba's beachhead AND to its broader volunteer-org ambition, with one-line rationale each. Note where a competitor is a beachhead threat, a long-term-market threat, or an emerging/watch-list threat.

6. **Differentiation impact** — explicitly state which of Memba's hypotheses are **weakened**, **unchanged**, or **strengthened** by these newly-surfaced competitors. Be willing to tell us our "fair pricing" wedge is dead and that we must win on the domain model (households + activities + waivers + privacy + volunteer handoff) instead.

## Research standards

- Prioritize evidence over speculation; include **source links** for every pricing and feature claim.
- Distinguish **facts vs estimates vs interpretation**, and state a **confidence level** for key claims.
- Flag contradictions and anything you could not verify.
- Do not assume generic nonprofit/fundraising software fits volunteer membership orgs — interrogate memberships, households, renewals, and (for the outdoor beachhead) trips and waivers specifically.
- Pricing, funding, and feature sets change fast (especially for young entrants); **date your findings** (research conducted 2026).
