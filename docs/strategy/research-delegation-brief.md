# Research Delegation Brief: Memba Market and Competitors

## Context

We are researching the market for Memba, a SaaS membership platform for volunteer-run clubs, starting with outdoor/activity clubs such as mountaineering, hiking, skiing, cycling, paddling, climbing, and trail-running clubs.

Memba’s possible MVP includes:

- Member database
- Renewals
- Payments
- Family memberships
- Private content
- Member directory
- Admin roles
- Google Sheets export/sync
- Announcements

Later features may include:

- Trip/activity management
- Member-to-member communication
- AT Protocol / federated social identity
- Mobile app

## Main research question

Is there a viable market for Memba: a simple membership, renewal, communication, and private-content platform for volunteer-run clubs, starting with outdoor/activity clubs?

## Research tasks

Conduct deep market and competitor research. Compare existing solutions including:

- Wild Apricot
- ClubExpress
- MemberPlanet
- Join It
- Memberful
- Raklet
- Mighty Networks
- WordPress membership plugins
- Squarespace/Wix member areas
- Google Groups
- Mailchimp
- Discourse
- Meetup
- Eventbrite
- Google Sheets + Stripe/manual workflows

For each competitor, evaluate:

- Pricing
- Target customer
- Features
- Strengths
- Weaknesses
- Customer complaints
- Market positioning
- Suitability for volunteer-run clubs
- Suitability for outdoor/activity clubs

Pay special attention to:

- Volunteer-admin usability
- Family memberships
- Renewals
- Recurring payments
- Member directories
- Private content
- Email communication
- Member-to-member communication
- Event/trip management
- Data export/import
- Google Sheets integration
- Mobile friendliness
- Privacy controls
- Roles/permissions

Also research the outdoor/activity club market:

- Typical club sizes
- Membership dues
- Current tooling
- Pain points
- Whether these clubs appear willing to pay for software
- Federation or umbrella organization channels
- Common website, renewal, and communication patterns

## Required deliverables

### 1. Competitor matrix

A structured comparison across the listed competitors, including pricing, features, target customer, strengths, weaknesses, and fit for Memba’s target market.

### 2. Market overview

A summary of the outdoor/activity club market:

- What kinds of clubs exist?
- How many are there?
- How large are they?
- What common workflows do they have?
- What software do they appear to use?
- What budget/willingness-to-pay signals are visible?

### 3. Pain-point evidence

A ranked list of validated pains with source links and, where possible, direct quotes.

Look for evidence around:

- Existing software being too expensive
- Existing software being too complex
- Poor fit for volunteer admins
- Family membership limitations
- Email deliverability problems
- Migration pain
- Data export/import issues
- Poor support
- Website/private content limitations
- Event/activity management gaps

### 4. Pricing benchmark

Compare pricing models across competitors:

- Per member
- Per club/account
- Monthly vs annual
- Setup fees
- Transaction fees
- Payment processing fees
- Feature tiers
- Nonprofit discounts

Recommend pricing hypotheses for Memba for:

- Small clubs
- Medium clubs
- Large clubs
- Federations or umbrella organizations

### 5. MVP feature recommendations

Classify potential Memba features as:

- Table stakes
- Nice-to-have
- Differentiator
- Dangerous distraction
- Post-MVP

Features to classify:

- Member database
- Renewal tracking
- Recurring payments
- Family memberships
- Member self-service
- Admin dashboard
- Member directory
- Private content
- Email announcements
- Member-to-member email/list
- Event/trip management
- Waivers/liability
- Roles/permissions
- Import/export
- Google Sheets sync
- Mobile friendliness
- Multi-club tenancy
- AT Protocol/federation

### 6. Positioning recommendations

Evaluate these positioning hypotheses:

1. Simple membership software for volunteer-run outdoor clubs.
2. Replace your club spreadsheet, mailing list, and renewal process.
3. Membership, renewals, announcements, and trips in one place.
4. A private digital home for your club.
5. The modern alternative to Wild Apricot for activity clubs.

Recommend 3–5 positioning angles with evidence for or against each.

### 7. Key risks and validation experiments

Identify the riskiest assumptions and recommend validation experiments.

Examples:

- Landing page test
- Club admin interviews
- Pricing interviews
- Clickable prototype
- KMC pilot
- Competitor switching interviews
- Email deliverability spike
- AT Protocol research spike

## Research standards

- Prioritize evidence over speculation.
- Include source links for claims.
- Distinguish facts, estimates, and interpretation.
- Call out confidence levels.
- Highlight contradictions or uncertainty.
- Avoid assuming that generic nonprofit software fits outdoor/activity clubs without evidence.

## Desired final synthesis

End with a concise recommendation answering:

1. Is Memba likely to have a viable market?
2. What target segment should it start with?
3. What is the strongest MVP wedge?
4. What competitors are most threatening?
5. What should be validated before building further?
6. Should AT Protocol be part of MVP, later strategy, or dropped?
