# Zeffy competitor note

Zeffy was not mentioned in the three imported research briefs, but it is highly relevant and should be added to the competitive landscape.

## Source pages checked

- https://www.zeffy.com/
- https://www.zeffy.com/home/membership-application-form-nonprofits-associations
- https://www.zeffy.com/home/ticketing-system-fundraising-events

Checked on 2026-05-25.

## Positioning

Zeffy positions itself as “100% free fundraising software” for nonprofits. Its homepage says it is trusted by 50,000+ nonprofit organizations across North America.

Its model is unusual: Zeffy says it charges nonprofits no platform fees and covers transaction fees, relying instead on optional contributions from donors/supporters at checkout.

## Relevant features

Zeffy offers several features that overlap with Memba’s proposed MVP:

- Membership forms.
- Membership database.
- Multiple membership types.
- Custom questions on membership forms.
- Automatic renewals.
- Member-managed renewals.
- Electronic membership cards.
- Expiry dates on membership cards.
- Tax receipts.
- Credit card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, ACH/PAD, and cheque support.
- Import/export of member information.
- Contact and transaction history.
- Event ticketing.
- Custom ticket types and questions.
- E-tickets.
- QR-code check-in via phone.
- Participant reports.
- Reminder emails and follow-up emails.
- Donor/contact CRM.
- Newsletter/email tooling.

## Why Zeffy matters

Zeffy changes the pricing benchmark. For price-sensitive clubs, especially nonprofits, “free including payment processing” is a much stronger anchor than Wild Apricot, ClubExpress, Join It, or MemberPlanet.

It may be a serious competitor for clubs whose core needs are:

- Collect membership dues.
- Sell event tickets.
- Track supporters/members.
- Send receipts.
- Avoid software and payment fees.

For small clubs with very low dues, Zeffy may be a “good enough” option that makes a paid Memba subscription harder to justify.

## Likely gaps for Memba to investigate

Based on public marketing pages, Zeffy appears nonprofit/fundraising-first rather than outdoor-club-operations-first. Important open questions:

- Does Zeffy properly model households/families as first-class groups, or only membership types/forms?
- Can one household pay while each person has an individual profile, waiver status, emergency contact, and activity participation history?
- Does it support member-only directories with privacy controls?
- Does it support private website/content access based on active membership?
- Does it support trip leader roles, participant caps, waitlists, approval workflows, emergency contacts, and waiver checks?
- Does it support member-to-member communication or mailing-list style workflows?
- How does the optional-contribution checkout feel to members paying club dues?
- Are there eligibility restrictions: registered nonprofits only, country restrictions, or verification requirements?
- How do clubs feel about a third-party donation prompt at checkout?

## Strategic implication for Memba

Memba should not compete with Zeffy on generic payments, donation forms, basic membership forms, or zero-cost fundraising.

Memba’s differentiation must be sharper:

- Outdoor-club-specific household model.
- Membership status tied to private content, directory access, and activity/trip participation.
- Trip/activity workflows for leaders and participants.
- Waivers and emergency contacts per individual participant.
- Volunteer-admin handoff and Google Sheets sync.
- A calmer club-operations product, not a fundraising platform.

Zeffy should be added to the competitor matrix as a high-priority “free fundraising/membership/event tool” competitor and a major pricing-risk benchmark.
