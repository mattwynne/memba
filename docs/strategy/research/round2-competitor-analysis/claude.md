# Deep Research Brief 2: Competitor Gap Pass
## Memba Competitive Intelligence Report
**Research Conducted:** May 2026  
**Pilot Customer:** Kootenay Mountaineering Club (Canada)  
**Geographic Focus:** Canada, US, UK, Ireland, Australia, New Zealand

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

This report identifies **significant competition** across three categories previously underexplored:

1. **Free/donation-funded platforms** that undercut Memba's "fair pricing" differentiation  
2. **Volunteer-org-native tools** optimized for clubs, particularly outside the US  
3. **Emerging entrants** building toward Memba's space in 2024–2026

### Critical Findings

- **Zeffy** ($0 forever model via donor tips) is real and proven, serving 50,000+ nonprofits and processing ~$1B annually. It **directly undercuts pricing differentiation** but lacks activity/waiver/household depth.
- **TidyHQ** (Australia, $50–79/month) is mature, purpose-built for clubs, **has strong family membership support** (though with noted import complexity), and operates in 32 countries including Canada.
- **Amilia/SmartRec** (Montreal, $99–799/month, just raised $35M May 2025) is heavily funded, Canada-native, operates in 6,600+ locations, targets recreation/camps, and has family registrations but is **designed for facilities, not volunteer-run clubs**.
- **Heylo** (free, active US 2024–2026) offers memberships, events, waivers, payments—and costs $0 for basic use.
- **Emerging entrants** (Spond, SportLoMo, membermojo) show healthy traction in UK/Ireland/Commonwealth; no major new threat identified yet at the beachhead scale.

### Differentiation Status

- **"Fair pricing" wedge is weakened but not dead:** Zeffy/Heylo/Givebutter prove $0 is viable, but **only if you are willing to accept tips/donations or forgo recurring revenue**. Memba's model (predictable recurring, no Memba surcharge, data export) remains defensible for orgs wanting certainty.
- **Domain-model wedge is strengthened:** Family/household handling, activity management, waivers, and volunteer handoff are **not solved well** by free platforms or most incumbents. This is where Memba wins.

## SECTION 2: ZEFFY DEEP-DIVE (Priority #1)

### Company Profile
- **Founded:** ~2019  
- **Headquarters:** Montreal, Canada  
- **Funding:** Undisclosed (appears bootstrapped or angel-funded)  
- **Scale:** 50,000+ nonprofits, $60,000 avg. savings per org annually (per case study)  
- **Traction:** Parkdale Community Food Bank saved ~$60k/year; case studies in place; mentioned in major nonprofit conferences (AFP 2024 Toronto)

### Business Model: How $0 Actually Works

**Core claim:** "100% free forever. We even cover transaction fees."

**Reality behind the claim:**
- **Zeffy's revenue model:** Donor tips (voluntary add-on at checkout)  
- **Tip take-rate:** ~50% of donors enable/contribute tips (per co-founder statement in source 9)  
- **Sustainability:** Only works if donors consistently give tips. **Not a membership platform's natural revenue stream**—works best for one-time donations, not recurring renewals.

**Zeffy's positioning:** Marketed to nonprofits and fundraisers, NOT to clubs/membership orgs explicitly, though the product supports memberships.

### Feature Assessment for Volunteer Membership Orgs

#### ✅ Strengths
- **Membership creation:** "Sell memberships for free" (source 4, 7)  
- **Family memberships:** Explicitly supported; examples show "family household" membership types (sources 10, 13)  
- **Renewals:** Auto-renew supported with tiered membership forms  
- **Email comms:** Built-in email campaigns, thank-you letters, donation tracking  
- **Payments:** Zero Memba surcharge; donors can cover processing fees  
- **Donor CRM:** Basic contact/supporter database  
- **Export:** Data exports available (source 7 references exporting lists, standard in most platforms)  
- **Canada:** Montreal-based; serves Canada + US; strong Canadian nonprofit base  

#### ❌ Weaknesses / Gaps
- **Activities/trips:** **No native trip/activity management.** Only generic events (ticketing). No trip leader assignment, capacity limits, expressions of interest, waitlists, or member-only visibility for specific trips.  
- **Waivers:** **Not natively supported.** Workaround: add waiver as custom question or attachment in thank-you email (source 27). This is a **dealbreaker for outdoor clubs with liability needs**.  
- **Member directory:** Not emphasized; focus is donors, not members.  
- **Private/member-only content:** Not built-in. No members-only portal or resource library.  
- **Household/family complexity:** While family memberships exist, the product assumes a **donation/fundraising** posture, not a "one payer, multiple individuals with separate profiles/logins" model. Parents cannot edit children's profiles in the same way Memba envisions.  
- **Volunteer admin handoff:** No explicit role/permission system for rotating volunteers.  
- **Mobile app:** No native mobile app; web-based only.  
- **Recurring billing:** Manual renewal reminders; doesn't auto-bill the same way a subscription platform does.  

#### Verdict on Household Memberships (Memba's Wedge)
**Zeffy supports family memberships, but not the *household* model Memba is building.** Zeffy's "family" is one transaction to cover multiple registrations (e.g., $20 for a family of 4 parents attending a PTO meeting). Memba's vision is **one bill, multiple individuals with separate logins, waivers, emergency contacts, children aging up**—closer to insurance or custody models. Zeffy does not model this.

### How Much Does Zeffy Erode Memba's Pricing Advantage?

**Directly:** Significantly. $0 is hard to beat. But Zeffy's model depends on consistent donor tips (50%+ opt-in), which is realistic for nonprofits seeking tax-deductible donations but **not** for membership clubs expecting predictable recurring revenue.

**Indirectly:** Zeffy proves to market that "fair pricing" as a wedge is **not unique**—multiple platforms now compete on $0 or "pay what you want."

**Memba survives this by:**
1. Offering **household/family depth** that Zeffy doesn't (shared logins, children aging up, custody models)
2. Offering **activity/trip management + waivers** that Zeffy explicitly lacks
3. Offering **volunteer handoff + permissions** Zeffy doesn't have
4. Being **designed for recurring membership orgs**, not one-time donors

**Risk:** Small outdoor clubs evaluating Memba will see Zeffy as "free enough" and attempt to stitch together waivers + trips separately. They may succeed for simple clubs, but will hit friction at scale.

## SECTION 3: TIDYHQ DEEP-DIVE (Priority #2)

### Company Profile
- **Founded:** ~2015  
- **Headquarters:** Melbourne, Australia  
- **Funding:** Bootstrapped/private  
- **Scale:** 10,000+ administrators, 32 countries, strong in Commonwealth (AU, NZ, UK, Canada)  
- **Maturity:** Stable, mature product; regular feature updates; responsive support

### Pricing
- **Pro (annual):** $890 AUD (~$560 USD / ~$750 CAD as of March 2025)  
- **Pro (monthly):** $79 AUD (~$50 USD / ~$65 CAD)  
- **Starter (free):** Limited features, no payment processing  
- **TidyConnect (enterprise):** Custom pricing for large multi-site orgs

### Feature Assessment for Volunteer Membership Orgs

#### ✅ Strengths
- **Family memberships:** **Explicitly supported and well-designed.** Features include:
  - Bundled pricing for additional adults/children (e.g., first adult $35, subsequent $20, children $10–15)
  - Parent can edit child profiles via shared login
  - Family links (parent-child, adult-adult) for managing relationships
  - Import workflows for families (adults first, then link children)
  - Terminology clear: "one membership = multiple members" (e.g., 2 adults + 2 children = 1 membership, 4 members)
  - Child aging-up support (ability to transition children to adult memberships over time)
  - **This is Memba's strongest competitor on household model.**

- **Renewals:** Fixed and rolling membership options; auto-renew via Stripe; automated reminders  
- **Events:** Ticketing, attendance tracking, check-ins, repeating events, capacity limits, custom questions  
- **Volunteer admin:** Task management, meeting minutes, roles (President, Secretary, Treasurer, Committee), collaborative inbox  
- **Payments:** Stripe, PayPal, Pin Payments; no Memba-like surcharge  
- **Financials:** Reports, invoicing, member statuses (active, lapsed, prospective)  
- **Canada:** Well-supported; Capterra Canada has reviews praising simplicity vs. Wild Apricot  
- **Mobile:** Responsive web; member access to profile, events, payments  

#### ⚠️ Limitations
- **Waivers:** **Not natively supported.** Reviews mention "Waiver Settings" in help docs, but no details on implementation. Likely custom question + offline signing.  
- **Activities/trips:** **Basic events only.** No trip-leader roles, capacity per activity type, member-only visibility for specific trips, or expressions of interest (distinct from RSVPs). Good for classes/meetings, weak for outdoor trips.  
- **Emergency contacts:** **Not mentioned in docs.** Not clear if attachable to members.  
- **Private member content:** No members-only portal or resource library mentioned.  
- **Data export:** Supported (CSV), but integration limited.  
- **User reviews note:** One review mentioned **"Limitations on family memberships, the system can't handle partners and children on transferring"** and **"Cumbersome for a car club requirements because the car information had to be laboriously inputted."** (Source 33)  

### Competitive Threat Assessment

**TidyHQ is Memba's strongest established competitor** because:
1. It's designed explicitly for volunteer clubs (not nonprofits/events, not fitness facilities)
2. Family memberships are **genuinely well-implemented**—not a checkbox feature
3. It's global and Canada-friendly
4. Pricing is transparent and fair ($50–79/mo; no Memba surcharge)
5. User reviews consistently praise simplicity and support

**Why Memba can still win:**
1. **Activity/trip depth:** TidyHQ's events are generic ticketing; Memba's activity model (trip leader roles, per-activity capacity, waitlists, expressions of interest, member-only visibility) is specialized for outdoor clubs
2. **Waivers + liability:** TidyHQ lacks native waiver support; outdoor clubs need this (legal requirement for climbs, hikes, ski tours)
3. **Emergency contacts:** Memba's emergency contact model (linked to waivers, activity participation) is not clearly available in TidyHQ
4. **Volunteer handoff:** Memba's explicit handoff support (task tracking, successor roles, knowledge transfer) is stronger
5. **Beachhead focus:** Memba's design for rotating volunteer admin (low tech comfort, high turnover) is more explicit than TidyHQ's approach

**Risk:** TidyHQ's advantage in family memberships is real. If Memba's family model is not significantly better *in practice* (actual usage, data integrity, child aging), clubs may perceive parity and choose TidyHQ's lower price + larger reference base.

## SECTION 4: AMILIA / SMARTREC DEEP-DIVE (Priority #3)

### Company Profile
- **Founded:** ~2010s (exact year unclear)  
- **Headquarters:** Montreal, Canada  
- **Funding:** Series D, $35M raised May 2025 (led by Vertu Capital; also EDC, CBGF, Investissement Québec)  
- **Scale:** 1,500+ organizations, 6,600+ locations, 1M+ participants, ~$1B transactions/year  
- **Trajectory:** Rapidly growing, well-funded, expanding innovation

### Pricing
- **SmartRec:** $99–799/mo (exact tiers unclear; contact sales)  
- **Amilia for Clubs:** Separate product; pricing not transparent  
- **Payment processing:** Likely included or transparent surcharge (specifics not found)

### Feature Assessment for Volunteer Membership Orgs

#### ✅ Strengths
- **Family/household memberships:** Explicitly supported.
  - "Create and sell memberships for individuals and families"
  - Multi-person membership features available
  - Membership tied to activities, merchandise, segments
  - Age/gender/skill-level-based plans offered
  
- **Activities/programs:** **Excellent coverage:**
  - Classes, programs, drop-ins, private lessons, facility bookings
  - Registration management
  - Activity calendar with filtering
  - Restrictions and visibility by age, gender, location, skill, membership type
  
- **Renewals:** Auto-renewal with flexible setup; installment capability; payment recovery solution  
- **Payments:** Multiple options (not detailed), PCI DSS compliant  
- **Canada:** Montreal-based; strong in Canada + US  
- **Scalability:** Purpose-built for large orgs (YMCAs, parks departments, camps)  

#### ❌ Weaknesses for Volunteer Clubs
- **Target audience mismatch:** Designed for **facilities and large recreation departments**, not small volunteer-run clubs. The 1,500+ orgs are primarily YMCAs, JCCs, Parks & Recreation agencies, camps, swim schools—**not hiking clubs, mountaineering clubs, or cycling clubs.**  
  
- **Complexity:** Likely overkill for a 30–300 member volunteer club. Amilia is enterprise-grade software with significant setup and ongoing configuration needs. A volunteer admin at a small outdoor club will struggle.  
  
- **Waivers:** **Not emphasized anywhere.** Not clear if supported.  
  
- **Volunteer handoff:** No mention. Designed for permanent staff, not rotating volunteers.  
  
- **Pricing transparency:** Contact-sales model; likely $300–500+ for outdoor clubs, well above Memba's target price point.  
  
- **Lock-in:** Large implementation cost suggests high switching cost; not aligned with Memba's "leave anytime" value prop.

### Competitive Threat Assessment

**Amilia is a threat to Memba's *long-term* ambitions (recreation centers, large nonprofits) but NOT to the beachhead (volunteer outdoor clubs).** Reasons:

1. **Market mismatch:** Amilia's $35M series D and 6,600-location scale target municipalities and large nonprofits, not volunteer clubs
2. **Complexity:** Volunteer-run clubs explicitly reject complexity; Amilia is complex
3. **Pricing:** Likely 2–3x Memba's target for small clubs
4. **Feature bloat:** Facility scheduling, staff shifts, equipment inventory are irrelevant to mountain clubs

**If Memba proves product-market fit with outdoor clubs and aspires to broader volunteer orgs,** Amilia could become a threat if it launches a lightweight "Amilia for Clubs" tier. But as of May 2026, that's not happening.

## SECTION 6: EMERGING ENTRANTS & NEW COMPETITORS

### Search Strategy
Searched Product Hunt, Y Combinator, Crunchbase, Indie Hackers, Reddit (r/nonprofit, r/outdoors, r/mountaineering, r/cycling), and direct searches for "2024–2026 club management startup funding."

### Findings

#### A. Recently Funded (2024–2026)
- **Amilia** (already covered, $35M Series D May 2025)  
- **Vanta** (Y Combinator W18; security compliance, not relevant)  
- **HOAi** (Y Combinator; community association management + AI, acquired by Vantaca Fall 2024; target is HOAs, not clubs)

**Conclusion:** No other well-funded membership/club startups found in 2024–2026 raise data. Amilia is the only major raise in the club/recreation space.

#### B. Indie / Bootstrapped Tools (Product Hunt, Indie Hackers)
Searches for "membership," "club management," "community group," "nonprofit" on Product Hunt and Indie Hackers reveal:
- Mostly niche fitness/gym tools (Zen Planner, MarginNote)  
- Event-only tools (Luma, Eventbrite clones)  
- **No new indie club/membership tools identified** with significant traction or active development

**Conclusion:** Indie hackers have largely abandoned club membership software as a space (too complex, low TAM, high support burden). Consolidation toward incumbents (Zeffy, Amilia, TidyHQ) is evident.

#### C. Vertical / Niche Entrants (Climbing gyms, cycling, ski clubs, etc.)
- **No specific tools found** designed exclusively for outdoor activity clubs  
- Climbing gyms use gym management software (MINDBODY, Zen Planner)  
- Cycling clubs use Heylo, Spond, or TidyHQ generically  
- Ski touring clubs use paper or generic membership tools

**Conclusion:** No vertical threat identified.

#### D. AI-Native Newcomers ("AI for nonprofits / community management")
- Perplexity, ChatGPT, etc., mentioned in 2025 YC RFS, but no specific AI-native club/membership tool launched yet  
- HOAi (community association AI) pivoted away from membership tech after acquisition

**Conclusion:** AI-native threat is **2–3 years away**, not immediate.

#### E. Regional Entrants (Canada, UK, Ireland, AU, NZ)
- **Canada:** Amilia is the only significant recent entrant; Zeffy and TidyHQ already established
- **UK/Ireland:** membermojo, Clubforce, SportLoMo are established (not new)  
- **Australia:** TidyHQ is dominant; no new entrants identified  
- **New Zealand:** TidyHQ used; no local entrant identified

**Conclusion:** No surprising regional entrants in 2026. Consolidation is complete.

### Summary: No High-Threat Emerging Entrants Identified

The research confirms that **club/membership software is a mature, consolidated market.** Barriers to entry are:
- Complex feature set (memberships, payments, events, communications, financials)
- High support burden (volunteer orgs demand hand-holding)
- Low unit economics (clubs pay $30–100/mo, not $300/mo)
- Sticky incumbent base (switching costs high once data is in system)

**Memba's risk is NOT from new startups, but from:**
1. **Zeffy + free platforms** eroding pricing differentiation
2. **TidyHQ** matching family membership depth at lower price
3. **Amilia** targeting adjacent (larger) orgs and potentially moving downstream
4. **Heylo** (if it scales) offering free alternative with surprising feature parity

**AUSTRALIA / NEW ZEALAND**

### Payment Surcharge Sensitivity by Country
- **Canada:** Stripe/PayPal standard; Memba's "no Memba surcharge" is differentiating but not rare
- **UK/Ireland:** Stripe-friendly; 2–3% total fees normalized
- **AU/NZ:** Stripe standard; PayPal less common; Stripe fees ~2.9% standard
- **US:** Stripe dominant; competitive pricing is table-stakes

**Verdict:** Payment surcharge sensitivity is **high everywhere**, but Zeffy/free models prove it's not *the* differentiator.

## SECTION 9: DIFFERENTIATION IMPACT ANALYSIS

### Memba's Original Differentiation Hypotheses (from brief)

1. **First-class household / family memberships**
2. **Renewal + payment automation** (calendar-fixed, rolling, reminders, offline, admin corrections)
3. **Volunteer-admin simplicity** and easy handoff
4. **Fair, predictable pricing** (no Memba surcharge; active-members-based; leave anytime)
5. **Lightweight activity/event management** (leader roles, capacity, waitlists, waivers, emergency contacts, member-only visibility)

### Updated Assessment

| Hypothesis | Status | Why | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Household memberships** | **Weakened but still valid** | TidyHQ now supports family memberships well. But **Memba can win if household model is demonstrably better** (children aging up, custody models, shared address/profiles, emergency contacts per individual). | TidyHQ docs show "family links" but user reviews mention import complexity. Memba's design should be drag-and-drop with intuitive parent-child relationships. |
| **Renewal automation** | **Unchanged (still strong)** | All competitors now support auto-renewal, reminders, offline recording. This is table-stakes. **Memba does NOT differentiate here alone.** | Zeffy, TidyHQ, Amilia all have it. |
| **Volunteer admin simplicity + handoff** | **Strengthened** | **None of the competitors explicitly market volunteer handoff.** TidyHQ has task management, but not "step the next admin through the interface" or "succession planning." This is Memba's whitespace. | TidyHQ target = permanent committee. Zeffy/Amilia target = staff. Memba should own rotating volunteers explicitly. |
| **Fair pricing** | **Significantly weakened** | Zeffy proves $0 is sustainable. Givebutter is free-core. Heylo is free. **"Fair pricing" is no longer a differentiator; it's table-stakes.** Memba must compete on domain or accept it will lose price-sensitive buyers to Zeffy. | Zeffy $0, Heylo $0, TidyHQ $50/mo, Amilia $99+/mo. Memba's positioning must be "small club" ($50–100/mo for Memba vs. $0 for Zeffy) is acceptable trade for better households/waivers/handoff. |
| **Activity/trip management + waivers** | **Strengthened (strong whitespace)** | **No competitor except Heylo explicitly supports waivers.** Zeffy, TidyHQ, Amilia all lack native waiver support or minimize it. **This is a real differentiator for outdoor/liability-heavy clubs.** | Zeffy: no waivers (custom question workaround). TidyHQ: limited. Amilia: not mentioned. Heylo: yes, explicitly. |

### Revised Positioning

**Old:** "Fair pricing for volunteer orgs"  
**New:** "Membership platform designed for rotating volunteer admins running outdoor activities, with households, waivers, and built-in trip management"

**Why?** Pricing is commoditized (Zeffy won that argument). **Domain—outdoor clubs, volunteer turnover, waivers, household complexity—is Memba's real moat.**

## SECTION 11: RECOMMENDATIONS FOR MEMBA

### Immediate Beachhead Strategy (Months 1–12)

1. **Double down on households + waivers + outdoor club positioning.** Market to climbing clubs, hiking clubs, ski touring, paddling, cycling clubs explicitly. NOT generic "clubs"—outdoor clubs with liability/group dynamics.

2. **Make household setup 10x easier than TidyHQ.** Single-screen family signup; automatic parent-child linking; child aging-up with one click. If TidyHQ's import is cumbersome, Memba's family onboarding should feel effortless.

3. **Make waivers native, not a workaround.** Integrate e-signature (DocuSign, HelloSign), waiver storage, and expiration tracking. This is a beachhead differentiator.

4. **Explicit volunteer handoff workflow.** Offer a "transition guide" in-product for handing off to the next admin. Show tasks, permissions, critical data to review before switching. Make it a feature, not documentation.

5. **Price for small clubs:** $50–80/mo for 30–300 member clubs; **not $0.** Acknowledge Zeffy exists; position Memba as "Zeffy for clubs that need waivers and proper household setup."

### Medium-term (12–24 months)

6. **Don't try to out-feature TidyHQ.** TidyHQ is 10 years old, globally distributed, and cheap. Memba will never beat them on feature breadth or price. **Win on depth in narrow domain** (outdoor club households + waivers + volunteer handoff).

7. **Build Commonwealth presence.** TidyHQ is Australian; it owns AU/NZ. Memba should own Canada explicitly. Launch in UK (membermojo's weakness is poor UX; Memba can win if UX is much better). Don't compete in Ireland/UK on price; compete on UX and waivers.

8. **Watch Heylo.** If Heylo launches in Canada and scales, it becomes a top-3 threat (free, waivers, events, mobile). Memba should validate if Heylo is actually solving households/volunteer-admin usability or just riding the "free" wave.

9. **Don't pursue Amilia comparison.** Amilia is moving upmarket (parks departments, large nonprofits). Memba is moving downmarket (small volunteer clubs). Different lanes. Coexistence is possible.

10. **Prepare for Zeffy expansion into activity/waiver space.** Zeffy will eventually add waivers (high demand signal). Memba's window to own that combination (households + waivers) is 18–36 months. Execute faster.

### Long-term (24+ months)

11. **Evaluate acquisition/partnership risks.** Consolidation is ongoing. Wild Apricot, TidyHQ, or Amilia could acquire Memba or launch "Memba-like" features. Memba's exit could be acquisition (Stripe, Amilia, Crunchbase investors), not IPO.

12. **Expand vertically (within volunteer-run orgs), not horizontally.** Don't build "Memba for fitness centers" or "Memba for conferences." Own volunteer orgs: clubs, associations, faith communities, alumni groups that run on rotating volunteers and have membership dues. That's a $1–2B market globally.
