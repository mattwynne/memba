memba

Navigation & pages · working proposal · May 2026

One small system, split cleanly across three audiences.

The behaviour we want already exists in the codebase — it's just all on one unauthenticated page. This proposal keeps the MVP scope tight and re-homes each action onto the surface that owns it: a public site for visitors, an internal back-end for Memba staff, and — for members — each club's own white-label site, with Memba kept out of sight.

Visitors · Memba staff · Club members
Audience 01

Visitors

Strangers who land on the homepage and might become customers. They read what Memba is and, if interested, ask for an account. They never create anything themselves — yet.

Public · responsive · memba.io
Audience 02

Memba staff

Us. We set up each club by hand for now — create the club, add its members — until self-serve sign-up and subscriptions exist. A plain internal tool, built for speed.

Internal · laptop · /admin (sign-in required)
Audience 03

Club members

People in a club that's already set up. They sign in on their club's own website — club logo, club colours, club domain — read what others have posted, look up fellow members, and send a note to the whole club. Most never learn Memba exists.

White-label · iPad-first · the club's own domain
01 — The brand boundary

Members never meet Memba.

Memba is the platform that runs in the background. Two surfaces wear Memba's own brand. The third — everything a member ever touches — wears the club's brand: their domain, their logo, their colours. To a member, this just is their club's website. The line below is the most important decision in this whole document.

Memba's brand

Until uploads & theming controls exist: a club site can't look unstyled. Every new club ships on a sensible default theme — the club's name set as a clean wordmark, a neutral default brand colour (a calm slate, deliberately not Memba's forest), and the standard layout. Picking a colour, then uploading a logo, then custom imagery are progressive upgrades on top of that floor — the page is always presentable, never a blank skeleton.
02 — Identity & access

One sign-in. Two planes. A switcher on only one of them.

Everyone signs in the same way — an emailed magic link, no passwords. What you can reach is worked out after sign-in, and where you are decides what you see. A person can belong to several clubs, be Memba staff, or both.

The club plane

Each club's own domain

kootenaymc.ca · white-label

Here you're simply “a member of this club.” No Memba, no other clubs, no switcher — that would leak the platform and your other memberships. One club per door. This is what keeps the illusion intact for the 80-year-old: his club's site is just his club's site.

The Memba plane

memba.io

your account hub + (for staff) the back-end

Here you're knowingly inside Memba, so a club switcher belongs. Sign in and you land on “My clubs” — every club you can reach — and step into any one. Staff (a @memba.io address) also get the /admin back-end from here.

Authorization is derived, not assigned. Same magic link everywhere; after sign-in Memba checks the email: a @memba.io domain ⇒ staff; an active membership ⇒ member of that club. The switcher lives only on the Memba plane — a club's own domain never shows other clubs. Stepping from the hub into a club hands you a session on that club's site, and from there it's just their website. Wrong door fails closed and quietly: “If you're a member, check your inbox.”

07 — Held back, on purpose

What's deliberately out of this cut.

The brief is small on purpose. These are real and wanted — just not in the first simple site. Keeping them off the map now is what makes the three surfaces clean.

Not yet — soon

  • Logo & image upload club sites run on a default theme until then
  • Self-serve brand controls colour & domain set by the club, not staff
  • Self-serve club sign-up staff create clubs by hand for now
  • Subscriptions & billing the reason sign-up stays manual
  • Renewals & dues membership status, grace periods

Later — bigger bets

  • Club-built public pages their homepage, edited by the club
  • Events & trips sign-ups, leaders, waitlists
  • Member-to-member email list replies fanned back out
  • Google Sheet sync for the membership secretary
  • AT Protocol & social profiles the open question from the brief
The shape of “in, for now”: a Memba-branded page that turns interest into a request, a Memba-branded back-office where we set clubs up by hand, and a white-label club site — on the club's domain, in the club's colours — where members read the noticeboard, look each other up, and send a note to everyone. Most of this already half-exists in the codebase; the work is moving it to the right door, adding magic-link sign-in, and wrapping the member-facing surface (and its emails) in a per-club brand layer that falls back to a clean default theme.
