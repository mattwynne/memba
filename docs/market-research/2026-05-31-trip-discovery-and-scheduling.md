# Trip discovery and scheduling concerns

**Source:** An anonymized club email thread, 28–31 May 2026

## Summary

Members want a reliable, browseable way to find and join trips well before they happen. A failing email-list workflow makes the problem acute, but the underlying need is broader: people with work and family commitments need to see enough detail, reserve a place, and plan ahead without winning an email-response race.

## Customer context and job

A visible cohort of newer, often younger members is keen to progress from social climbing events to mountaineering. Some have children, busy schedules, and limited knowledge of local routes. Their job is to discover suitable trips early, commit with confidence, and gradually learn enough to participate—or eventually lead—safely.

## Pain points

### Trips are hard to plan around

- Members report too few mountaineering trips visible on the schedule.
- Last-minute or “pop-up” trips do not work for people balancing work and family; they want to secure plans one or two months ahead.
- Larger trips need earlier promotion, but an email sent in advance misses people who join afterwards.
- Registration feels like a race: wait for a leader’s message, reply quickly, and hope a place remains.

### Email is an unreliable single point of failure

- Multiple members say they have not received trip notifications for months, including reports of delivery problems for particular email providers.
- The service’s reported status conflicts with members’ experience, creating uncertainty about whether messages are reaching anyone.
- Word of mouth is carrying discovery for at least some well-attended events, rather than the club’s official pre-event message.
- Members perceive the issue as urgent and longstanding; it risks undermining confidence in the club’s value.

### New members are not yet ready to lead

- The desire for more member-led trips is shared, but newer members may be young, new to the area, and unfamiliar with routes.
- Lack of scheduled trips deprives them of the experience and local knowledge that could help them become future leaders.
- Existing leaders can be reluctant to post trips when personal schedules are uncertain, because they do not want to cancel.

## Desired experience

- A simple, public, easy-to-navigate trip calendar.
- A detailed page for each trip, available as soon as the trip is posted.
- Sign-up from the trip page, rather than through a first-come email exchange.
- The ability to browse only the trip types a member cares about.
- Enough advance notice to arrange work, family, travel, and equipment.
- Clear, trustworthy communication when a delivery problem is known.

## Suggested solutions from the thread

1. **Make the calendar the source of truth.** Publish trips with descriptions and registration as early as possible; use email to point members back to the listings, not as the only place a trip exists.
2. **Offer a recurring digest.** An automated monthly “upcoming trips” email could complement the calendar and provide a useful interim measure.
3. **Provide an immediate fallback while email is unreliable.** Use a conventional group email or another known-working channel until the list service is repaired, while recognising that email alone does not solve long-range discovery.
4. **Be transparent about delivery status.** Tell members what is known, what is being tested, and where they can reliably check trips in the meantime.
5. **Support more leaders.** Pair uncertain leaders with co-leaders and help newer members learn routes and trip-leading practices over time.
6. **Favour simplicity over a bespoke clone.** A comparable club’s custom event site was praised for its experience, but was also reported to require substantial build effort and ongoing volunteer maintenance. The capability—not a custom implementation—appears to be the need.

## Evidence of demand and success signals

- A climbing event attracted roughly two dozen members, including younger adults and some families, after being promoted on social media and listed on the schedule.
- Attendees reportedly expressed interest in more mountaineering trips.
- Members reacted positively to examples of simple calendars and varied scheduled social events.

## Representative anonymized voice

- “Pop-up trips don’t work” for people who need to plan around busy lives.
- Members want to “secure a spot” when they see a good trip far enough in advance.
- A comparable calendar was valued because it was “very easy to navigate” and “very simple.”

## Confidence and limitations

This is a single, internally circulated thread, not a representative survey. The attendance and attendee feedback are reported second-hand by one participant, so treat the size and composition of the affected cohort as directional. Email-delivery failure is corroborated by several independent members in the thread; the broader demand for advance planning and a calendar-based workflow has medium confidence and merits validation with a wider member sample.
